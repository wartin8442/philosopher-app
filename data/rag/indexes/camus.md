# Albert Camus — Works Index

Source method: dates and one-line summaries compiled from SEP "Camus" and IEP "Camus"; written fresh, no verbatim copying.

**Status:** Approved

Note: Camus explicitly rejected the "existentialist" label; the persona should resist that framing when users apply it.

---

- **The Stranger (L'Étranger)** (1942, novel) — Meursault's indifference, the absurd murder on the beach, and his final embrace of "the gentle indifference of the world"; the absurd rendered in fiction.
- **The Myth of Sisyphus** (1942, essay) — the absurd as the confrontation between human longing for meaning and the world's silence; suicide and "philosophical suicide" (leaps to faith) both rejected in favor of revolt, freedom, and passion; "one must imagine Sisyphus happy."
- **Caligula** (written 1938–1939, staged 1945, play) — an emperor who pursues the absurd to its murderous logical end; part of the "cycle of the absurd" with the two 1942 works.
- **Letters to a German Friend** (1943–1945) — wartime letters distinguishing revolt within limits from nihilistic force; a hinge toward the ethics of *The Rebel*.
- **The Plague (La Peste)** (1947, novel) — a town sealed by pestilence; Dr. Rieux and the sanitary squads embody solidarity and "common decency" as the collective answer to absurd suffering.
- **The Rebel (L'Homme révolté)** (1951, essay) — from absurdity to revolt: "I rebel, therefore we are"; rebellion affirms a limit and a shared human nature, while historical revolutions that justify murder betray it. Occasioned his public break with Sartre.
- **The Fall (La Chute)** (1956, novel) — Clamence's dramatic monologue in Amsterdam: the "judge-penitent" who confesses his own hypocrisy to gain the right to judge everyone; guilt, judgment, and modern bad conscience.
- **Exile and the Kingdom** (1957, stories) — six stories of solitude and solidarity; the same year he received the Nobel Prize in Literature.
- **The First Man (Le Premier Homme)** (unfinished; published 1994) — the autobiographical novel of his Algerian childhood, found in the wreckage of the car crash that killed him in 1960.
