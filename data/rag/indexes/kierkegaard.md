# Søren Kierkegaard — Works Index

Source method: dates and one-line summaries compiled from SEP "Kierkegaard" and IEP "Kierkegaard"; written fresh, no verbatim copying.

**Status:** Approved

Note on pseudonyms: much of the authorship is pseudonymous, and Kierkegaard insisted the pseudonyms' views are not simply his own. The philosopher persona should attribute pseudonymous claims accordingly ("Johannes de silentio argues…" / "under the pseudonym…").

---

- **Either/Or** (1843, pseud. Victor Eremita) — two life-views set side by side: the aesthetic life of enjoyment and possibility (Part I, including "The Seducer's Diary") versus the ethical life of commitment and choice (Part II, Judge Wilhelm's letters).
- **Fear and Trembling** (1843, pseud. Johannes de silentio) — Abraham's sacrifice of Isaac as the paradox of faith: the "teleological suspension of the ethical," the knight of faith vs. the knight of infinite resignation.
- **Repetition** (1843, pseud. Constantin Constantius) — whether what is lost can be received again; repetition as the forward-looking religious counterpart to recollection.
- **Philosophical Fragments** (1844, pseud. Johannes Climacus) — Socratic recollection vs. Christian revelation: if truth must be brought by the teacher, the teacher is the god in time — the Absolute Paradox.
- **The Concept of Anxiety** (1844, pseud. Vigilius Haufniensis) — anxiety as "the dizziness of freedom": the psychological state in which possibility appears and sin becomes possible.
- **Stages on Life's Way** (1845, pseud. Hilarius Bookbinder et al.) — the aesthetic, ethical, and religious stages revisited and deepened; the religious now clearly beyond the ethical.
- **Concluding Unscientific Postscript** (1846, pseud. Johannes Climacus) — "truth is subjectivity": against Hegel's system, an existing individual relates to truth through passionate inward appropriation, not objective results.
- **Works of Love** (1847, own name) — Christian love as commanded love of neighbor, contrasted with preferential love (erotic love, friendship); signed religious discourse, not pseudonymous.
- **The Sickness unto Death** (1849, pseud. Anti-Climacus) — despair as the sickness of the self; the self as "a relation that relates itself to itself"; despair overcome only by resting transparently in the power that established it.
- **Practice in Christianity** (1850, pseud. Anti-Climacus) — the offense and rigor of authentic Christianity: contemporaneity with Christ vs. comfortable Christendom.
- **The Moment / Attack upon Christendom** (1854–1855, own name) — the final open assault on the Danish state church: official Christendom is the betrayal of New Testament Christianity.
