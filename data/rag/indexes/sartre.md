# Jean-Paul Sartre — Works Index

Source method: dates and one-line summaries compiled from SEP "Sartre" and IEP "Sartre"; written fresh, no verbatim copying.

**Status:** Approved

---

- **The Transcendence of the Ego** (1936) — the ego is not *inside* consciousness but an object *for* it; consciousness itself is impersonal, spontaneous, and empty.
- **Nausea** (1938, novel) — Roquentin's discovery of brute contingency: existence is superfluous, unjustifiable, "de trop"; the philosophical novel of his early period.
- **The Imaginary** (1940) — imaging consciousness posits its object as absent or unreal; imagination reveals consciousness's freedom to negate the given.
- **Being and Nothingness** (1943) — his major philosophical work: being-in-itself vs. being-for-itself, the nothingness at the heart of consciousness, bad faith (the waiter), the look of the Other, radical freedom and responsibility.
- **No Exit (Huis clos)** (1944, play) — three characters locked together after death; source of "hell is other people," dramatizing the objectifying look of the Other.
- **Existentialism Is a Humanism** (1945 lecture, published 1946) — the popular statement: "existence precedes essence," abandonment, anguish, and the forlorn freedom to invent values. *(Caution: a simplified popularization; Sartre later expressed reservations about it.)*
- **Anti-Semite and Jew** (1946) — analysis of antisemitism as bad faith: the antisemite chooses hatred to flee their own freedom and contingency.
- **Saint Genet** (1952) — existential biography of Jean Genet: how a person makes something of what has been made of them.
- **Critique of Dialectical Reason** (1960) — the later attempt to reconcile existentialist freedom with Marxism: praxis, scarcity, the practico-inert, and how groups form and ossify.
- **The Words (Les Mots)** (1964) — autobiography of his childhood among books; written around the time he was awarded (and declined) the Nobel Prize.
