# Thomas Aquinas — Works Index

Source method: dates and one-line summaries compiled from SEP "Aquinas" and IEP "Aquinas"; written fresh, no verbatim copying.

**Status:** Approved

---

- **Commentary on the Sentences** (c. 1252–1256) — his first major theological work, a systematic commentary on Peter Lombard covering the whole range of doctrine; many positions later refined in the Summa appear here first.
- **De Ente et Essentia / On Being and Essence** (c. 1252–1256) — short early treatise on what essence is and how it relates to existence; source of the essence/existence distinction and the argument that in God alone essence and existence are identical.
- **Disputed Questions on Truth (De Veritate)** (1256–1259) — disputed questions on truth, knowledge (human, angelic, divine), providence, and free choice; his most detailed epistemology.
- **Summa Contra Gentiles** (c. 1259–1265) — a systematic case for Christian claims arguing as far as possible from natural reason alone, without presupposing Scripture; Books I–III cover God, creation, and providence philosophically, Book IV the revealed doctrines.
- **Disputed Questions on Evil (De Malo)** (c. 1266–1272) — on evil as privation, sin, and free choice; Question 6 is his fullest defense of free will against determinism.
- **Summa Theologiae** or **Summa Theologica** (1266–1273, unfinished at his death) — his masterwork, a complete ordered summary of theology in question/article form. Part I: God and creation (the Five Ways are ST I, Q.2, a.3); Part I-II: human action, habit, law (natural law is I-II, Q.94); Part II-II: the virtues in detail; Part III: Christ and the sacraments.
- **Commentaries on Aristotle** (c. 1267–1273) — close commentaries on the *Nicomachean Ethics*, *Metaphysics*, *Physics*, *De Anima*, and others; the workshop where his Aristotelianism is built.
