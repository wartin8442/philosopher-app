# Friedrich Nietzsche — Works Index

Source method: dates and one-line summaries compiled from SEP "Nietzsche" and IEP "Nietzsche"; written fresh, no verbatim copying.

**Status:** draft — Approved

---

- **The Birth of Tragedy** (1872) — Greek tragedy arose from the union of the Apollonian (order, form) and Dionysian (chaos, intoxication) drives, and was killed by Socratic rationalism; art, not reason, justifies existence.
- **Untimely Meditations** (1873–1876) — four essays against the culture of the new German Reich, including "On the Uses and Disadvantages of History for Life" (too much historical consciousness paralyzes action) and the Schopenhauer and Wagner essays.
- **Human, All Too Human** (1878; expanded 1879–1880) — the turn to his "free spirit" period: aphoristic, scientifically minded debunking of metaphysics, religion, and morality as human, historical products.
- **Daybreak (Morgenröte)** (1881) — continues the campaign against morality, treating moral feelings as inherited custom; groundwork for the later genealogical method.
- **The Gay Science** (1882; Book V added 1887) — the madman's proclamation that "God is dead" (§125) and the first statement of eternal recurrence (§341, the demon and the hourglass); joyful skepticism as a way of life. *(Common misattribution: the madman passage is here, not in Zarathustra.)*
- **Thus Spoke Zarathustra** (1883–1885) — his poetic-prophetic centerpiece: the Übermensch, the death of God, will to power, and eternal recurrence dramatized through the prophet Zarathustra.
- **Beyond Good and Evil** (1886) — critique of past philosophers' dogmatism; the world seen from inside as will to power (§36); master morality vs. slave morality first sketched (§260).
- **On the Genealogy of Morality** (1887) — three essays tracing the origins of "good/evil" out of ressentiment, of guilt and bad conscience out of debt, and of the ascetic ideal's grip on humanity; his most systematic book.
- **The Case of Wagner** (1888) — polemic against Wagner as the artist of decadence.
- **Twilight of the Idols** (written 1888, published 1889) — compact demolition tour: "How the 'True World' Finally Became a Fable," the four great errors, Socrates as symptom of decline.
- **The Antichrist** (written 1888, published 1895) — full-force attack on Christianity as a morality of pity that inverts natural values.
- **Ecce Homo** (written 1888, published 1908) — ironic intellectual autobiography ("Why I Am So Clever"), reviewing his own works.
- **The Will to Power** (posthumous compilation, 1901/1906) — *not a book Nietzsche wrote or authorized*: notebook fragments assembled by his sister Elisabeth Förster-Nietzsche. Cite the notebooks (Nachlass) cautiously and never as if this were a finished work.
